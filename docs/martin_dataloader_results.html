Data-loader speedup: what changed and what is in memory

Batch preparation (disk to yield batch) is now fast enough that training waits on the GPU, not on data, at HEALPix level 5 and level 6. Model inputs are unchanged, loss stays within run-to-run noise, and everything is opt-in through data_loading.optimizations.

What changed

  • Even worker split. The last loader worker used to get 12 samples while the others got 8, so every run ended with ~40 s stalls. Spreading the remainder removed them (−28% on its own).
  • Reader flags. Read only the variables and columns that are used, once, without extra copies (prep −11%).
  • Tokenizer flags. Skip coordinate encodings, index sorts and padding copies that training discards (prep −11%).
  • bf16 source tokens. Workers cast source tokens to bf16 before handing them over. The model's embedding engine already casts them to bf16 first, so its input is bit-identical, while each sample in shared memory halves. Needed at level 6. Measured with my prototype flag; the team version is Alejandro's source_tokens_in_mixed_precision (branch alejandro/bf16-worker), so it is left out of my PR.

Use config/dphpc_dataloader_all.yml (level 5) or config/dphpc_dataloader_level6.yml (level 6) on branch martin/main. Each flag is documented in docs/martin_dataloader_flags.md.

Every flag and setting we tried

All optimizations sit under data_loading.optimizations and default to off, so old configs behave exactly as before. Effects are at level 5 against the profiled baseline jnsctk9d (per-sample prep 40.0 s per worker, 4.67 s/step) unless noted. The reader and tokenizer flags were measured as two groups of four, so their effects are per group; no single flag inside a group was measured on its own.

Every sample as an allocation, effect by effect

This is the same view as PyTorch's memory_viz Active Memory Timeline, but for the loader on training rank 0. Each coloured band is one finished sample. It appears when a worker hands it over to /dev/shm, is as tall as the sample, and disappears when training takes it as the next batch. Bands are stacked in hand-off order, so the stack drops when a sample is consumed. Pick a step to see what each change did. The axes stay fixed per level. Hover a band for its details.

How to read it. A tall stack means many finished samples are queued, so training never waits. Gaps on the floor mean nothing is queued and training is waiting for a worker. The right edge of the last band is when the 100th batch reached training. Sizes are what a sample takes in /dev/shm. At level 5 they are measured per sample in va54ajr5 (fp32 runs counted with source tokens doubled back). Samples a run does not share with that run use the mean (38 of 100 in runs without the even split). Level-6 sizes are estimates: 9.2 GiB in fp32 and 4.69 GiB in bf16. Times come from the per-sample profiler and the training-loop wait log. Batches are matched to workers in PyTorch's round-robin order.

What this means for the model side

  • The GPU now sets the pace at both levels. Further speed has to come from the model step; the loader has headroom only while per-sample prep (~100 s per worker at level 6, mostly ERA5 target building and tokenizing) stays below workers × step time.
  • bf16 source tokens are safe to send. At level 6 the mean per-step loss difference was 0.0001 on identical samples. The level-6 GPU peak also fell from 82.2 to 77.7 GiB, most likely because the model's own fp32→bf16 cast no longer makes a copy.
  • Level 6 is tight on memory. GPU memory peaks at 78–83 GiB per GH200, and the node's HBM was 359 of 380 GiB in use. With 9 workers the node ran down to 0.02 GiB available, which is why the recommendation is 8.
  • bf16 in the workers goes in through Alejandro's source_tokens_in_mixed_precision, which follows mixed_precision_dtype. Until it is merged, the level-6 config uses 5 workers (22.1 s/step); with it, 8 (13.9 s/step).
